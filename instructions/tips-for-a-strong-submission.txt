Pick a real customer scenario. Anchor your build to a specific industry and a specific problem. Generic builds score low.
Lead with the outcome. The first thing the evaluator reads should explain what business problem you solved and what value it creates. Save the architecture for later.
Show the journey, integrated. Disconnected demos stitched together is the most common failure mode. Make the data flow obvious: raw data (synthetic or publicly available) through Lakeflow to Unity Catalog to Lakebase, made intelligent with ML or Gen AI and queryable through a Genie Agent, surfaced in a business app.
Document trade-offs. Why this approach and not another? A short "decisions and trade-offs" section signals expertise.
Quantify the value. "Saves $2M annually" beats "improves efficiency." Put a number on it, even if estimated.
Use AI as a force multiplier. Document the tools, prompts, and patterns you used. The FE Bar rewards builders who treat AI as a teammate.
Rehearse the roleplay. A strong build with a weak live demo still loses Customer Skills points. Practice your tell-show-tell out loud, keep it tight, and be ready to handle objections from both AI personas, the business stakeholder and the technical one.
